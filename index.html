About

I'm an Assistant Professor of College of Information Sciences and Technology at the Pennsylvania State University. My research focuses on security, privacy, and trustworthy generative AI systems.

  • Secure and Efficient AI Inference Systems: Developing algorithms and system-level techniques to ensure reliable behavior during inference, with a focus on efficiency-aware security, as well as scalable deployment and trade-offs between trustworthiness, utility, and efficiency.
  • User-Centered Trust and Real-World AI Risks: Understanding and mitigating real-world harms from AI systems, including unsafe content generation (featured in MIT Technology Review), online trust, and privacy risks in health care.

Previously, I received my Ph.D. in the Department of Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University, where I was advised by Dr. Yinzhi Cao. I also worked closely with Dr. Neil Gong from Duke University during my doctoral study.
