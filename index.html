About

I will be joining the College of Information Sciences and Technology at the Pennsylvania State University as an assistant professor in July 2025. My research spans security, privacy, and AI, with a current interest in trustworthy generative AI systems:

  • Algorithms, Theory, and Infrastructure: Developing foundational algorithms and scalable systems to ensure trustworthiness, including attacks, provable defenses for large language or vision models, and trade-offs between trustworthiness, functionality, and efficiency.
  • Applications and User-Centered Perspectives: Addressing real-world societal challenges posed by generative AI systems, including unsafe content generation (featured in MIT Technology Review), privacy risks in health care, and online trust.

I recently defended my Ph.D. in the Department of Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University, where I was advised by Dr. Yinzhi Cao. I also worked closely with Dr. Neil Gong from Duke University during my doctoral study.

Openings: Seeking Ph.D. students, research interns, and visiting scholars. If you’re interested, please read this.
